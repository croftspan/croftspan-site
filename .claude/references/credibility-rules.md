# Credibility Rules — PE Review Compliance

Source: ~/projects/gigo/docs/gigo/briefs/2026-03-29-credibility-overhaul.md

## The Standard

Honesty as a credibility strategy. The PE reviewer's strongest reactions were to transparency, not polish. Lead with what's real and specific. The numbers are strong enough to not need inflation.

## Stat Rules

1. Every stat on the site must trace to a specific experiment with a specific number.
2. No extrapolating from one test case to a universal claim.
3. Separate "validated in controlled evals" from "used in production."
4. Present results as achievements, not grades. "First-pass quality so high reviewers found nothing to fix" not "97% convention compliance."

## Domain Claim Rules

1. Only claim domains that have eval data OR documented real-world usage.
2. Separate language: "Validated in controlled evals across X and Y. Used in production across A, B, and more."
3. Dogfooding and real deployment anecdotes are worth more than another percentage point on an A/B eval.

## Founder Framing Rules

1. "Built by Eaven at Croftspan" not "Built at Croftspan."
2. The solo-founder-with-AI narrative is rarer, more credible, and more compelling than anonymous company framing.
3. Enough context that people know this isn't a weekend experiment. Not the full resume. Blockfolio ($150M exit), ToppsNFTs ($500M exit), 15 years of building from zero to scale.
4. The story: "One experienced operator who's been doing this pattern his entire career with human teams, and is now translating it to AI teams."

## The Meta-Insight

"You're not building a developer tool. You're building the operational playbook for how one person runs an AI-native company."

The credibility overhaul isn't about fixing bad marketing. It's about letting the real story show through instead of hiding behind anonymous polish.
