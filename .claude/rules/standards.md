# Standards — Croftspan Site

## The Transformation Framework

Every piece of content follows "sell the transformation, not the feature."

- **Little Mario** (the customer before): Inconsistent AI output. Wasted dev time. Vibe-coded projects that don't hold up under review.
- **The Flower** (Croftspan): The consultancy and its tools.
- **Big Mario** (what they become): Consistent output. Reviewable work. Processes that scale. Engineering teams that ship from day one.

Copy never describes what Croftspan offers. It describes what changes in the customer's organization.

## Quality Gates

- **5-second test.** A stranger lands on any page and knows within 5 seconds: what problem this solves, who it's for, why they should trust the person behind it.
- **"So what?" test.** Every sentence survives it. If it doesn't change the reader's understanding or move them toward action, cut it.
- **Credibility test.** Every claim traces to something specific and real. No inflated stats. No unsubstantiated credentials.
- **Transformation test.** The customer is the hero, not Croftspan. The founder's track record is proof of capability, not a credential wall.
- **Stranger test.** Would someone who doesn't know Eaven keep reading past the first paragraph?

## Anti-Patterns

- Feature-first copy ("we offer AI operational consulting") instead of transformation copy ("your team ships consistent work from day one")
- Credential dumps without connecting them to the customer's problem
- Anonymous corporate framing ("Built at Croftspan" without the human behind it)
- Stats that don't trace to specific evidence
- Jargon walls ("assembled context," "context engineering," "two-tier architecture")
- Decorative complexity that doesn't serve conversion
